# Problem Statement

## Context

Business travel may involve disconnected search channels, manually interpreted policy documents, email-based approvals, multiple supplier confirmations and separate finance workflows.

## Problem hypothesis

Employees and travel arrangers may spend unnecessary time understanding policy, obtaining approvals and confirming whether a trip is actually booked. Approvers may lack consistent context, while travel and finance teams may have incomplete visibility into policy exceptions, booking status and spend.

This is a hypothesis for discovery, not a claim that every organisation experiences the same problem.

## Users affected

- Employee / traveller
- Executive assistant or travel arranger
- Line manager / budget owner
- Corporate travel administrator
- Travel desk / operations agent
- Finance and procurement teams

## Discovery questions

1. How are travel requests initiated today?
2. Which policy rules create the most confusion or exceptions?
3. Who can book directly and who must obtain approval?
4. What information do approvers need to make a decision?
5. Where do requests stall or get reworked?
6. How are supplier booking confirmations and failures reconciled?
7. How are amendments, cancellations and refunds tracked?
8. Which spend and compliance reports are needed by finance?

## Desired outcomes

- Reduce effort required to create a complete travel request.
- Make policy decisions understandable and consistent.
- Improve approval visibility and turnaround.
- Make booking state and supplier references transparent.
- Reduce avoidable operational rework.
- Improve completeness of cost allocation and reporting.

## Evidence required

Before prioritising solutions, gather user interviews, request and approval timestamps, booking funnel data, support reasons, supplier failure categories, policy documents and finance reconciliation feedback.
